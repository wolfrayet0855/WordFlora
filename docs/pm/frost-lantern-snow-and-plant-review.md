## 1. The white plant: the snowdrop on the pack card

**Which plant.** The only white plant the owner can see in Frost & Lantern on a device is the **snowdrop** in the pack
card (`Assets.xcassets/Art/pack_frost_lantern_card.imageset/pack-card-*.svg`). These files are byte-identical to
`design-preview/frost-lantern/pack-card-*.svg`, generated by `build.py` `snowdrop_full()`. It is a static image, so no
code state can make it droop: not `FadedBloom`, not thirsty or wilt, not hydration, not the faded-bloom `(dy,dx,xk)`
offsets. The droop is in the art pose itself. (If the owner meant a ladder plant instead, the only one drawn nodding
on purpose is Ghost Pipe. That would need a separate look; nothing in this review touches it.)

**Why it reads droopy** (numbers in the 200-unit plant box, `build.py` lines 416 to 448):

1. **A shepherd's-crook stem.** `snowdrop_stem` climbs to the left of the head (`hx - 14s`), turns about 180 degrees
   over the top (control point `hy - 16s`) and comes down onto the head. Each stem ends lower than its own top. In
   cartoon language, that hook is the "wilted flower" sign.
2. **The left stem curls back on itself.** Every stem comes from the head's left side, but the left head
   (70, 88) grows from a base at x 94. So that stem travels past the head and hooks back: the most wilted shape on the card.
3. **The bells hang straight down and stay closed.** The outer tepals sit at only ±28 degrees and nothing tilts, so
   each head is a closed drop under a hook. The right head (140, 104) hangs below where the leaves end.
4. **The leaves splay out and stay short.** The strap leaves end at (74, 104) and (128, 112), leaning out and ending
   halfway up the plant. They read as flopping over. Next to them the hellebore has upright stems, open faces turned
   up and dark palmate leaves, so the contrast makes the snowdrop look sick by comparison.
5. **Colour is not the cause.** The grey-blue edge `#768C91` and glaucous leaf `#6E9C7E` are true to the plant and pass
   contrast (3.3:1 on light ground). Desaturating or darkening them would not fix the pose, so the colours stay.

**Fix: pose only, in `build.py` (colours, sizes, layer ids and head count unchanged).**

- Lift the heads: `SNOWDROP_HEADS = [(118, 64, 1.0), (72, 80, 0.92), (138, 94, 0.88)]`.
- Make each stem rise in a smooth line and finish in a short arch from **its own side** (`d = ±1`), with no crook:
  arch top `(hx - 8sd, hy - 11s)`, ending on the ovary at `(hx, hy - 6s)`.
- Tilt each bell **38 degrees** out, away from its stem (so it nods outward rather than hanging), and open the outer
  tepals from ±28 to **±44 degrees**. The tilt is applied in each tepal's own rotate about `(hx, hy)` and as a
  `rotate()` on the bell group, so the `PlantThinning` pivots stay the head points.
- Make the leaves upright and taller: tips at (86, 94), (116, 100) and (100, 110), widths 4.4, 4.4 and 3.8.

## 2. Falling snow

### Where it belongs

| Place | Snow? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `PackCardArt` (Seasons page header **and** the Shed Seasons shelf: one mount covers both) | **Yes**, clipped to the card | The card is the pack's face. Snow over its sky is exactly the owner's ask and it costs one overlay |
| Plant page stage, when the plant's pot is one of `PotCatalog.packPots` | **Yes**, when there is no garden weather and no rain that day | The child sees what the pack pots do every day. Weather owns the sky: `wx == nil && !raining`, so a frosty night's sparkles or the rain never double up |
| Puzzle, Butterfly game, Speed Bloom | No | Play surfaces: motion there is a distraction |
| Quiet Garden | No | Owner rule for this pass (the Shed shelf is already hidden in Quiet Garden) |
| Pot picker and repot chips | No | Small tiles, many pots: snow would flicker over the grid |
| Home and Garden (G5 scene) | Later | The G5 scene spec says snowfall "comes from the weather layer"; reuse this same view there in G5 |

### The component: `SnowfallView` (`WordFlora/Components/SnowfallView.swift`, about 70 lines)

A copy of the `RainCanvas` pattern (`RainOverlay.swift:52`). It holds no state: every flake's place comes from the clock
and a hash (`WeatherDraw.rnd`), so pausing simply freezes it. `TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1/30, paused:))`
wraps one `Canvas`. There is one filled `Path` per depth layer, so a frame costs three fill calls.

```swift
struct SnowfallView: View {
    var count: Int            // card: 22; plant page: 18 (Calm: half)
    var still = false         // Reduce Motion (system or the app switch) or Low Power: one calm frame at WeatherArt.stillTime
    var paused = false        // off screen, app not active
    var groundY: CGFloat? = nil   // plant page: flakes fade out over the last 24 pt above the ground
    var layers: [Int] = [0, 1, 2] // plant page: [0] behind the plant, [1, 2] in front, as RainCanvas
}
```

Flakes are soft round dots, not six-armed glyphs. At these sizes a glyph turns into an asterisk (the Seasons glyph
lesson), and it would compete with the Snowflake indigo pot pattern and the two flakes already drawn on the card.

| Layer | Share | Radius | Fall speed | Side sway | Opacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| far | 50 % | 1.4 pt | 14 pt/s | ±4 pt, `sin(t * 0.6 + φ)` | 0.55 |
| mid | 33 % | 2.0 pt | 20 pt/s | ±6 pt | 0.75 |
| near | 17 % | 2.8 pt | 27 pt/s | ±8 pt | 0.90 |

- Position: `x = w * rnd(i, 1) + sway`, `y = (t * speed + rnd(i, 3) * (h + 10)) mod (h + 10) - 5`. The flake re-enters at the top
  at a new `x` each cycle (`rnd(i, cycle)`), as the rain does. There is no wind slant: the snow is gentle and straight down.
- A flake takes about 5.5 to 11 s to cross the 150 pt card and 11 to 21 s to cross a 300 pt stage. That is slow and calm.
- Calm mode: half the count and 0.7x the speed (the same idea as `MistSprayView`'s halved droplets). It stays on.
- Reduce Motion (system or `reduceMotionOverride`) and Low Power Mode: one still frame. The flakes stay visible but do not move
  (the same rule as `weatherStill` in `PlantDetailView.swift:194`).
- Paused when off screen, when the app is not active, or when `stageVisible` is false (plant page). The card pauses on `onDisappear`.

### Colours (WCAG contrast, flake at full / at 0.75 opacity)

| Flake | appBackground | appSurface | Card sky | Card snow bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light `#8FA9C4` (frost blue-grey) | 2.27 / 1.82 on `#FAF7F0` | 2.43 / 1.90 on `#FFFFFF` | 1.79 / 1.53 on `#D3DEEE` | 2.26 / 1.81 on `#F4F7FB` |
| Dark `#F4F8FC` (snow white) | 13.6 / 8.3 on `#1A2E1A` | 10.7 / 6.9 on `#2C3E2C` | 13.1 / 8.0 on `#1E2C46` | 2.28 / 1.90 on `#9AA7BC` |

- White flakes cannot work in light mode: they are 1.07:1 on `appBackground` and 1.00:1 on `appSurface`, so they would be
  invisible on the plant page. A white flake with a thin blue ring was tried in the review sheet. It read as bubbles, so it was dropped.
- `#8FA9C4` sits between `FL.ice` and `FL.iceRim`, so it belongs to the pack palette. The snow is decorative, so the
  3:1 rule for non-text graphics (WCAG 1.4.11) does not apply. The target is "visible but soft", and 1.8 to 2.4:1 keeps it
  behind the pots and plant.
- Pick the colour with `@Environment(\.colorScheme)`, as `WeatherPalette` does.

### Mounts (Swift, for the builder)

- `PackCardArt`: `Image(...).resizable().scaledToFit().overlay { SnowfallView(count: calm ? 11 : 22, still: still, paused: !visible) }`
  placed **before** `.clipShape(...)`. It keeps `.accessibilityLabel(PackCatalog.Copy.cardLabel)`. The overlay is hidden, so
  the label does not change.
- `PlantDetailView` stage ZStack (`PlantDetailView.swift:197`): `let snows = PotCatalog.packPots.contains { $0.id == plant.potId } && !app.settings.quietGarden && wx == nil && !raining`.
  Mount `SnowfallView(count:, still: weatherStill, paused: paused, groundY: groundY, layers: [0])` right after the
  `WeatherOverlay(.back)` slot, and `layers: [1, 2]` right after the `WeatherOverlay(.front)` slot. Both get
  `.frame(height: rowTop)`, as `RainCanvas` does. `paused` and `weatherStill` already exist there.

### Performance and battery

- At most 22 flakes, which is fewer than `RainCanvas`'s drops. Three `Path` fills per frame at a 30 fps cap, and no image or
  blur. The frame is not reused through `drawingGroup` (that is not needed at this size).
- Low Power Mode shows the still frame, so no timeline runs (`LowPowerWatch`). The view also stops when it is off screen or
  the app is in the background.

### Accessibility

`.accessibilityHidden(true)` and `.allowsHitTesting(false)`, as on every weather layer. There is no sound and no
announcement. Reduce Motion shows a still frame. The VoiceOver value of the plant page and the card label stay the same.

### Tests (`WordFloraTests/SnowfallTests.swift`; put the pure maths in a small `enum Snowfall` in `Rules/Snowfall.swift`)

1. `Snowfall.flakes(count:size:time:)` returns `count` flakes. Each one is inside `x 0...w` and `y -5...h+5`, and the result
   is deterministic for the same time.
2. Layer shares: 22 flakes split 11 / 7 / 4, and the total is always `count` (the same rounding as `RainCanvas.counts`).
3. `Snowfall.count(base:calm:)` halves the base under Calm and never returns less than 1.
4. `Snowfall.showsOnStage(potId:quietGarden:weather:raining:)` is true for each of the three pack pots and false for any
   other pot, in Quiet Garden, with any garden weather, and when it is raining.
5. Ground fade: a flake within 24 pt of `groundY` has opacity under 1, and below `groundY` it is not drawn.
6. UI audit: Seasons page and plant page in a Frosted glass pot, light and dark, with `-uitest-pack-owned`. Also check the still
   frame under Reduce Motion.

Effort: about 2 h, including the tests. This is a small, low-risk change: one new view, two mounts, no stored fields, and
it only shows for pack owners or on the Seasons card.

## Taste calls (defaults chosen; Fable can overrule)

1. Snow on the Shed shelf card as well as the Seasons page. Default yes: they share `PackCardArt`, and the shelf is 13+ only.
2. Dots, not flake glyphs. Default dots, for the reasons above.
3. Snowdrop tilt of 38 degrees and flare of 44 degrees. Lower values (about 25 and 38) move back toward the old, closed
   bell. The review sheet shows the chosen values.
