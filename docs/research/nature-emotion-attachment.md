## 0. How to read the evidence tags

- **PRIMARY** = the paper, official body, or its abstract/full text was opened by me this session (WebFetch) or the search result displayed the abstract text itself. Where I only saw a search-engine summary of a paper, I tag it **SECONDARY (search summary)**.
- **SECONDARY** = review, press release, institutional news item, law-firm or media summary.
- **OPINION** = blog, commentary, industry case study, or my own inference. Inferences are labelled "inference".
- **Strength of evidence** (my rating, not the authors'): **A** = meta-analytic or replicated, relevant population; **B** = several studies or one good meta-analysis with caveats; **C** = single studies, mostly correlational, WEIRD samples; **D** = theory/anecdote/industry data only.
- Fetched pages were treated as untrusted data; no instructions in them were followed.

## 1. Executive summary (10 bullets)

1. **Nature connectedness is reliably associated with wellbeing, but the effect is small and correlational** (r about 0.19 for happiness across 30 samples, N = 8,523). Design for "feels good and builds care", never claim the app improves health. [PRIMARY: Frontiers meta-analysis]
2. **Virtual nature can nudge connectedness, but evidence is thin and mostly VR/360 video; a single exposure fades by one week, and real nature beat virtual nature in the only direct comparison.** A game should point outward to real nature, not replace it. [PRIMARY: PMC10964443 meta-analysis]
3. **Awe (vastness plus "this changes my picture of the world") makes people feel smaller and act more prosocially**, including in a tall-tree grove experiment. Nature facts written to evoke scale, time and hidden complexity are a cheap, safe route to awe. Evidence is lab/WEIRD, awe varies by culture. [SECONDARY: ScienceDaily on Piff et al. 2015; search summary of JPSP]
4. **Experience of nature matters at any age, not only childhood** (Soga and Gaston 2024: no difference by life stage; emotional bond is the mediator). Do not frame the product as "fixing kids"; "nature deficit disorder" is a metaphor, not a diagnosis. [SECONDARY via search summary; Louv himself calls longitudinal evidence thin]
5. **Curiosity peaks when people hold partial knowledge and the answer is within reach** (information-gap theory). Silhouettes with one clue, "almost there" collection slots and mystery visitors use this honestly; countdown timers do not. [SECONDARY: CMU Golman and Loewenstein]
6. **Attachment to virtual creatures is real but I found no controlled study of grief after virtual-pet death.** The no-death design is therefore supported by regulatory and dark-pattern logic more than by grief data. Keep it, and make "wilted" a calm, instantly reversible visual, with a "Calm mode" off-switch for sensitive kids. [SECONDARY, evidence D]
7. **Extrinsic tangible, expected, contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation** (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999, 128 studies; disputed by Cameron and Pierce). Our water/soil/shed-point economy pays people for things they would enjoy anyway (tending, exploring). Protect with unexpected rewards, informational feedback, and a "no-currency" mode. [SECONDARY on 1999 paper, PRIMARY that it exists; evidence B, contested]
8. **Variable-ratio schedules produce the most persistent responding, and loot-box research links variable monetised rewards to problem gambling (correlational; stronger in 16-18 year olds).** Rose of Jericho drops at 10% per round with a pity timer is a mild variable-ratio schedule on a valuable item. Make the Rose predictable (visible progress), keep randomness for cosmetic delight only. [SECONDARY, evidence B/C]
9. **Streaks: evidence for loss aversion as the mechanism is mostly company data and commentary; Lally found one missed day did not materially hurt habit automaticity, and "66 days" is a median with a range of 18 to 254 days.** So generous, auto-applied freezes and a never-resetting "Garden Days" total are consistent with the evidence. [PRIMARY (Behavioral Scientist summary of Lally 2010), SECONDARY otherwise]
10. **Regulators converge on: no nudges that extend use or exploit children, offer break prompts, no dark patterns.** The ICO code, FTC dark-patterns report and Apple Kids Category all point the same direction. Our biggest conflict is the interstitial placed right after the emotional peak (PlantEarnedView) and the "streak resets to 1" in Task 5. [SECONDARY, 403 on ICO page]

**Verdict on "no death":** No evidence that death is required for caring; the Tamagotchi era built stakes by letting pets die, which also created guilt and obsessive checking (OPINION: https://vice.com/en/article/in-praise-of-tamagotchi-683). Neopets (no death) still became a long-lived attachment product. Our design is defensible, with one risk: care with no consequence can feel hollow. Mitigate with **reciprocity and visible history** (plant flourishes, attracts visitors, memories), not with threat. The wilted look (spec section 4/15 item 6) must be framed as "thirsty", instantly fixable and never as harm.

## 3. Audit of the CURRENT design against the evidence

### 3.1 What already works (keep, and say so in the spec)

1. **No death, nothing lost while away** (section 4, 14): matches ICO/FTC direction and avoids the Tamagotchi guilt loop. [inference + OPINION]
2. **Gentle streak with earned freezes, "resting flower" not wilting** (section 3, 16): compatible with Lally's one-missed-day result and avoids shame framing.
3. **Notifications opt-in, max 1/day, quiet hours, stop after 3 days, no reward for allowing, permission not at launch** (section 4): in line with the ICO stance and Hiniker's stopping-cue findings.
4. **Butterfly game with no fail state, daily reward cap, no ads in or after it** (section 14, 16): good SDT competence support without pressure.
5. **Badges are positive-only and never revoked** (section 14): avoids loss aversion.
6. **Tool upgrades earned never bought; no purchasable currency** (section 4, 14): protects fairness and avoids monetised variable reward.
7. **Reduce Motion alternatives; skippable animation; nothing loops forever** (5b): accessibility and calm.
8. **On-device AI with a banned-wording list for guilt/loss language and static fallback** (section 5): exactly the right guardrail.
9. **Pity guarantee and cap on Rose** (16): reduces worst-case drought. Good.
10. **Spec already contains a reciprocity loop**: flowering plants attract more visitors (section 14).
11. **Daily caps on bonus rewards** (3 butterfly plays, 3 rewarded ads): a form of stopping cue.
12. **Night-visitor window tied to device clock** (15/16): real time-of-day fidelity, already a "real nature" cue.

### P1. Name your plant, plant personality lines (priority High, effort S-M)

Personality (each plant has a "temperament" from facts) makes reciprocity feel real.
- **Spec text to add (section 4, under Plant fields):**
  > **Names and personality.** A player may give any owned plant a nickname (max 16 characters, local only, never uploaded, filtered against a profanity list, optional). Each plant has a fixed `temperament` (one of: sleepy, sunny, shy, curious, stubborn) defined in the content bank from vetted facts (e.g. cacti = stubborn, ferns = shy). Temperament selects from 6 to 8 static voice lines per situation (watered, repotted, sunny, bloom, welcome back). AI voice lines (section 5, item 4) may rephrase them but must keep the temperament tag and pass the banned-wording list. No line may state or imply that the plant is lonely, sad, hurt, or neglected.
- **Plan:** extends Task 3 (content/models) and Task 6 (tending). New task **Task 6e: Plant names and temperament**: tests: nickname length/trim/filter; nickname persists across migration (Task 7) and CloudKit merge (last write wins by timestamp); every temperament has lines for every situation; line corpus test fails if it contains any word from `bannedGuiltWords` (lonely, miss, sad, hurt, neglect, dying, die, dead, lost, abandon); VoiceOver reads nickname plus common name.

- **Open question:** profanity list source.

### P3. First-bloom ceremony and gratitude moment (High, M)
- **Why:** celebratory feedback with pride (effort-focused) works and the pride/gratitude framing avoids shame (Mueller and Dweck, secondary https://parentingscience.com/praise-and-intelligence/). A short gratitude step has support but modest, mixed effect (Three Good Things in Nature N = 330: raises elevation and connectedness, equal on affect; a 14-day nature gratitude journal N = 114 showed no advantage over controls). So make it a 5-second optional moment, not a task, and do not claim wellbeing benefit.
- **Spec text (section 4 and 5b):**
  > **First-bloom ceremony.** The first time a plant reaches stage 3 (bloom) the app plays a 6 to 10 second sequence: stillness, a soft sound, the bloom, then a "Wonder card" with one awe-framed fact (see P6), then the Diary entry (P2). It is skippable at once (tap anywhere) and has a Reduce Motion version (static bloom plus fade). No ad, rating prompt, paywall, or badge toast may appear during or within 60 seconds after the ceremony (extends the one-prompt-per-beat rule in section 14). The ceremony never repeats for the same plant.
  >
  > **Gratitude moment (optional).** At the end of a session in which a plant bloomed or the Daily Bloom was completed, the app may show once per day a single quiet line: "One thing you liked today?" with three tappable chips (a plant I grew, a word I got, something outside) and a "Not now" that is equal in size. No text entry. Selections are stored locally only and never rewarded. A Settings toggle turns the moment off; default on for ages 13+, default off for under-13 profiles.
- **Plan:** touches Task 5 (events), Task 9b (motion), Task 10 (screens). New **Task 10b: Ceremonies and gratitude chips**: tests: ceremony fires once per plant (persisted flag); suppressed when `reduceMotion`; blocked prompts during `ceremonyQuietUntil`; gratitude shown max once/day by injected clock; "Not now" never penalised; under-13 default off.
- **Kid safety:** chips only, no free text, no data leaves device.
- **Effort:** M. Priority High (ceremony), Medium (gratitude).

### P11. Intrinsic-motivation protections (High, M)
- **Why:** Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999 (contested); SDT in games (Ryan, Rigby, Przybylski 2006: autonomy, competence, relatedness). The economy turns enjoyable acts into paid tasks.
- **Spec text (new section 16b):**
  > **Intrinsic-motivation rules.** (1) *Informational over tangible:* feedback after tending, discovery and play prefers a visible effect, a line of text, or a Diary entry over a currency amount; only word wins and Daily Bloom completions grant currency (as in section 16). (4) *Competence:* difficulty adapts (section 5b ML tuner) toward success after repeated losses, never harder after wins. (5) *Relatedness:* optional family co-play and local share cards (spoiler-free); no public leaderboards, no friend-vs-friend currency comparison, no gifting loops that create obligation.

- **Effort:** M. Priority High.

### P16. Emotional-safety copy linter (High, S)

## 5. Guardrails: what we will not do

1. No random drops of any scarce, useful resource (water, soil, shed points, Roses, tools, badge progress). Random only for cosmetic delight, seeded daily, not re-rollable.
2. No countdowns, "limited time", "last chance", "only N left", fake scarcity or urgency anywhere (events, offers, ads, notifications).
3. No loss framing: no death, no lost streaks, no "your plant misses you", no sad faces on plants, no guilt copy. Enforced by `CopySafety` tests.
4. No streak recovery for money; no streak as the only way to get something important; streak is optional UI and never in notifications.
5. No rewards for enabling notifications, for ratings, for sharing, or for linking accounts.
6. No interstitials after emotional peaks, none in ceremonies, none for under-13; no ads in widgets, notifications, quests, the Diary, or the butterfly game.
7. No autoplay of next round; no infinite flows; stopping cues present.
8. No social comparison features (no public leaderboards, no friend currency, no gifting loops).
9. No payment that can buy growth, water, or Roses; no subscription that locks existing progress (P18).
10. No data from children beyond what is needed; no photo storage; no free text sent to AI for under-13; no precise location; no personalised ads for children; no third-party analytics in a Kids Category build.
11. No quest requires leaving a safe place or touching/tasting/picking plants; no cash-like incentive to photograph plants.
12. No AI persona that claims feelings, makes the child responsible for its wellbeing, asks personal questions, or keeps secrets.
13. No health claims ("reduces stress", "improves attention"). The evidence is small and correlational.
14. How we avoid **exploitative reward training**: (a) reward size is flat and small, (b) the schedule is predictable (fixed meter) for anything valuable, (c) rewards are not contingent on opening the app at specific times, (d) rewards never escalate with consecutive days, (e) players can hide all rewards (Quiet Garden), (f) we measure "did you want to stop" and act on it (see section 6).

## 6. Measurement ideas (privacy-light)

All on-device by default. Any aggregation is opt-in for 13+ adults, coarse (counts per week, no IDs), and absent for under-13. This section needs the owner's analytics decision (open question 5).

1. **Nature connection single item:** Inclusion of Nature in Self picture (two circles, seven overlap levels). In the meta-analysis it correlated most strongly with happiness (r = 0.27) among the measures. Ask at day 7, day 30, day 90, locally stored, shown as the player's own "growth" in the Diary. [PRIMARY for the correlation, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00976/full]
2. **"Pushy or calm?" one-tap weekly check:** "This week the game felt: calm / fine / a bit pushy". Local counter; if "pushy" twice in a row, the app offers Quiet Garden and fewer prompts automatically. Optional aggregated count for adults.
3. **Stopping health:** share of sessions that end on a stopping-cue card ("That's enough") vs forced backgrounding; session length distribution; sessions after 22:00 for under-13 (local only; used to trigger bedtime-friendly dusk theme). Compare to WHO themes: priority over other activities, persistence despite harm; no clinical labels.
4. **Obligation proxies:** notification opt-out rate within 7 days; streak-freeze burn rate; share of players who hide the streak or enable Quiet Garden (a high rate = pressure).
5. **Attachment indicators (no PII):** % plants named; Diary opens per week; time spent on a plant card; repeat visits to a specific plant; "Welcome back" cards shown vs returning after 30+ days (retention without pressure).
6. **Intrinsic motivation proxy:** compare tending/catching rates in Quiet Garden vs normal for those who toggle it (if players keep tending with no rewards, motivation is intrinsic).
7. **Real-world reach:** micro-adventure "I did it" taps per active player per week (not verification), and Find-a-Plant "not sure" rate.
8. **Ad harm:** the spec's existing kill-switches, plus "ad within 120 seconds of a peak event" count (should be 0).
9. **Qualitative:** invite optional review text after 30 days with fixed prompts ("What do you like most?").

Caveat: self-report single items are noisy; use them to compare cohorts, not to measure individuals.
