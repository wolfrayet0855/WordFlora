# Name stories (T-B)

Every plant card shows "How X got its name" when the plant has a `nameStory` in
`WordFlora/Resources/Plants.json`, otherwise the plain "Did you know?" fact. A story earns its card only when
it teaches something a child can link to the plant: a shape, colour, habit, a person who found it or a place.
Plain "X comes from Latin x, meaning X" stories were not used. Wording is grade 2-3 and passes `CopySafety`.
Plants named after a person or place use the botanical name and `language: "Latin"`, so the card's
"In Latin, it was ..." line reads well.

Confidence: **verified** = the meaning and the reason are both in the source; **safe** = the meaning is
sourced and the story only says what you can see, or hedges ("may", "no one knows for sure").
W = en.wikipedia.org/wiki/, Wt = en.wiktionary.org/wiki/. Sources for Rose, Fern, Orchid, Lotus, Bamboo,
Cherry Blossom, Corpse Flower (*titanum*), Dragon Blood Tree (*Dracaena*), Bird of Paradise, Passionflower and
Bleeding Heart were added by the integrator from standard botanical Latin; they are the pages to check in review.

## Changes from the research drafts

- Rose: Latin *rosa* replaced by *Rosa gallica*, "rose of Gaul" (the French rose).
- Fern: the old root *pornóm* replaced by *Nephrolepis*, "kidney scale" (the word reads badly for kids and is a far root).
- Orchid: *orkhis* glossed as "round root" was not a true translation; now *Phalaenopsis*, "like a moth".
- Lotus: "old Greek name" taught nothing; now *nucifera*, "nut bearing".
- Bamboo: Malay *mambu* is not settled; now *edulis*, "good to eat".
- Hibiscus: kept, plus the marshmallow root link.
- Cherry Blossom: the custom word *hanami* replaced by *serrulata*, "little saw teeth".
- Corpse Flower: "smelly flower" was not a true gloss of *bunga bangkai*; now *titanum*, "giant".
- Dragon Blood Tree: "red tree sap" was not a translation; now Greek *drakaina*, "she-dragon".
- Bird of Paradise, Silversword, Bleeding Heart, Passionflower, Rainbow Eucalyptus: botanical name with a real
  meaning instead of the English name repeated (or a religious story) in the chip.
- Lady's Slipper Orchid: draft explained *Cypripedium*; the app's plant is *Paphiopedilum*.
- Wollemi Pine: added that *nobilis* honours David Noble.
- Baobab, Welwitschia, Rafflesia, Giant Water Lily, Wollemi Pine, Puya: `language` "named after" changed to "Latin".
- Marigold: `language` "English" changed to "Middle English", origin "Mary + gold".
- Several lines shortened to pass the grade 3.9 reading check.
