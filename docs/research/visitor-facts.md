# Visitor facts (grade 2-3), with sources

Shipped in `WordFlora/Rules/Butterfly.swift` (`VisitorCatalog.all`). Rules: one or two short sentences, at most 16 words and 90 characters, short words, CopySafety clean, no death or danger talk. Tested in `ButterflyAndBadgeTests.testEveryVisitorHasAShortGentleFact`.

| Visitor | Fact | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Monarch | Monarch butterflies can fly up to 3,000 miles to a warm place for winter. | Eastern monarchs migrate up to about 3,000 miles to central Mexico (US Forest Service, "Monarch Butterfly Migration and Overwintering"; National Wildlife Federation). |
| Swallowtail | A swallowtail has long points on its back wings that look like tails. | The family is named for the tail-like extensions of the hindwings (Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Swallowtail butterfly"). Not every species has tails; the garden species we draw does. |
| Cabbage White | Baby cabbage whites are green caterpillars that love to eat cabbage leaves. | Larvae of *Pieris rapae* are green "imported cabbageworms" feeding on cabbage-family plants (University of Florida IFAS Featured Creatures). |
| Painted Lady | Painted ladies are found in more parts of the world than most butterflies. | *Vanessa cardui* is among the most widespread butterflies, on every continent but Antarctica and South America (Wikipedia; Butterfly Conservation UK). Hedged with "most". |
| Honeybee | Honeybees do a waggle dance to show other bees where to find flowers. | Karl von Frisch's waggle dance research (Nobel Prize 1973); the dance gives direction and distance to food. |
| Bumblebee | Bumblebees buzz to shake pollen out of some flowers, like tomato flowers. | Buzz pollination (sonication); bumblebees are used to pollinate greenhouse tomatoes (USDA Forest Service, "Buzz Pollination"). |
| Ladybug | Ladybugs eat tiny bugs called aphids. That helps keep plants healthy. | Most lady beetles prey on aphids and are used as biological pest control (University of Kentucky Entomology). |
| Dragonfly | Dragonfly ancestors flew in the sky about 300 million years ago, before dinosaurs. | Griffinflies (Meganisoptera), dragonfly relatives, lived about 300 million years ago in the Carboniferous; dinosaurs came about 230 million years ago (Smithsonian). Kept from the earlier corrected fact. |
| Blue Morpho | A blue morpho shines blue because tiny scales on its wings bounce light. | The blue is structural colour from microscopic scale ridges, not pigment (Natural History Museum, London). |
| Hummingbird | A hummingbird flaps its wings about 50 times a second. | Ruby-throated hummingbirds beat about 53 times a second; species range roughly 12 to 80 (Audubon; Cornell Lab "All About Birds"). |
| Firefly | Fireflies make their own light. They blink to talk to each other. | Bioluminescent flash patterns are signals between males and females (Smithsonian; Xerces Society). |
| Luna Moth | A grown-up luna moth has no mouth. It does not need to eat! | Adult luna moths have no functional mouthparts and do not feed (University of Florida IFAS Featured Creatures). The short adult life is left out on purpose. |
