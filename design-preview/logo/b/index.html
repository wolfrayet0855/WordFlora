Logo direction B: ring blooms

Each O stays a heavy Nunito Black ring, the same weight as the other letters, with its counter left open. Petals grow from under the ring, and a clear gap separates them from it. "Word" gets a six-petal amber daisy. "Flora" gets a five-petal coral blossom. Letters are converted to paths, so no web font is needed.

Monochrome

The mark uses one ink, #2C3E2C. The petals still read because a gap the colour of the background separates them from each ring. That gap is a mask, so the mark also works on photos and on any background colour.

Rationale

  • The O stays an O. The ring is the real Nunito Black "o", so it has the same weight and shape as the other letters. The petals are extra shapes behind it and never replace the letter, which keeps the word readable to early readers.
  • Two different flowers make a small garden. The Word O is a six-petal amber daisy, the app accent. The Flora O is a five-petal coral blossom. Neither has petals pointing straight sideways, so the letters can stay close together.
  • Colour is never the only cue. A clear gap around each ring outlines the petals in every colour mode, and the petals are lighter (light mode) or darker (dark mode) than the ring.
  • The icon is the Flora O. It is a cream ring with amber petals on the app's green. It keeps the cream, amber and green of the current smiling daisy, but now it is a letter as well as a flower. The five broad petals survive at 29 px, where the slimmer daisy breaks up into dots.
  • Recommendation: twin blooms. It is what the owner described and it is friendlier for kids. "Solo" is the fallback for widths of 120 px or less (see notes.md).
