Petal thinning

Daisy visibly thins as she loses love pips. Nothing here is redrawn: the page reads the app's own plant SVG, finds the petal rings, and hides real petals in a balanced order. A hidden petal is cloned, drifts down once through the rain and rests on the ground. When love comes back the petals grow out of the flower head one pip at a time, 0.3 s apart.

Try it

Daisy: 5 to 0 pips

Each pip takes a share of her 16 petals: 16, 11, 7, 4, 2, 0. The last day's petals lie on the ground. At 0 she is a bare yellow head on her stem for a moment, then she tucks into her pot as a seed.

Sunflower: two rings, same rule

44 petals in a back ring and a front ring. Both rings thin by the same share, so gaps go through both layers and the brown seed head shows as a bare head.

Saguaro: no petals to lose, so she droops

A cactus keeps its shape. Its body bends a little at the base and greys a little each day; the tiny flower on top still loses its seven petals. The sand stays put.

Rose: petals alone barely show, so she also droops

The rose is round blobs on a big dark base disc. Removing the five outer petals only trims the outline, so on its own it does not read as thin. Here it gets the same droop and grey as the cactus on top of the petal loss. Superseded by the rose rule: the outer ring peels, the bud unwraps, and the petals fall as curled rose petals. See rose-thinning.html and notes/rose-rule.md.

Why not a wedge mask

The same states made the other way: pie wedges cut out of the picture over the head, with a darker disc painted in the middle so the hole reads as a bare head. Left: element drop. Right: wedge mask.

All 50 plants, automatic, no hand work

5, 2 and 0 pips for each plant, straight from detection. Green: thins clearly. Amber: works but weak or odd, needs a look. Grey: no ring found, gets the droop treatment.
