Rose: peeling, not thinning

A rose loses its outermost petals first. Here the rose's big base disc is redrawn as five dark inner-row petals in the valleys between the outer petals, so each outer petal leaves together with the dark petal beside it, and nothing is left behind as a cup. The outer ring goes over two pips, then the bud unwraps layer by layer. The bud sits in green sepals. At 0 pips only the hip and sepals are left on the stem, and then the seed rests in its pot. Each falling petal is a curled rose petal in the colour of its own layer. It tumbles and sways as it falls.

Live

Six states

5: the full rose, the same as the art. 4: two outer petals gone, so the dark heart shows through the gaps. 3: a closed bud held by sepals. 2: the bud's wrap is gone, so a small swirl is left. 1: the last tight bud. 0: a bare hip and sepals on the stem. The last day's petals lie on the ground.

Falling petals

There is one sprite per layer, all in the rose's own colours. Each has a wide rounded top with a soft notch, a lighter rolled-back lip that gives the curl, and a narrow, darker claw at the base. The sprite's base is at 0,0 and it points up, so rotating it by the petal's angle points it outwards from the head.

Parts

The rule as layers, outer to inner; each tile shows one layer alone on the stem. Hidden: the r26 base disc. Added: five dark inner-row petals in the valleys (they replace the disc) and the sepal cradle.

The same rule on other plants

There is no peony, camellia, dahlia or ranunculus in the catalogue. These are the layered flowers that do exist. Only the rose has a true base disc under circle petals. Poppy (its cup disc) and rafflesia (its body disc) need the same "do not leave a cup" step. The rest only need the peel order: outer layer first, and whole layers at a time.
